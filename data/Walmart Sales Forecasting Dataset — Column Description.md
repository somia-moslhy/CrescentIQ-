# Walmart Sales Forecasting Dataset

## Dataset Overview

The **Walmart Recruiting - Store Sales Forecasting** dataset contains historical weekly sales information for **45 Walmart stores** located in different regions.

The main objective is to predict the **weekly sales of each department within each store** for a given week.

The dataset is divided into three main files:

- `train.csv` — Historical sales data, including the target variable `Weekly_Sales`.
- `test.csv` — Similar to `train.csv`, but `Weekly_Sales` is removed because it is the value we need to predict.
- `features.csv` — Additional weekly information such as temperature, fuel price, markdowns, CPI, and unemployment.
- `stores.csv` — Information about each store, mainly its type and size.

The competition evaluates predictions using **Weighted Mean Absolute Error (WMAE)**, where holiday weeks receive a higher weight.

---

## Column Descriptions

### 1. `Store`

**Description:**  
Identifies the Walmart store.

Each store has a unique numerical ID.

**Example:**

```text
1
2
3
...
45
```

**Type:** Categorical / Identifier

**Why is it important?**

Different stores can have different sales patterns because of their location, customer base, size, and store type.

---

### 2. `Dept`

**Description:**  
Identifies the department within a store.

A department represents a specific category or business area inside the Walmart store.

**Example:**

```text
1
2
4
7
...
```

The same department number can appear in multiple stores.

**Type:** Categorical / Identifier

**Why is it important?**

Different departments have completely different sales behaviors. For example, one department may have consistently high sales while another may have much lower sales.

---

### 3. `Date`

**Description:**  
Represents the week associated with the observation.

The sales are recorded on a **weekly basis**, so this column represents the week rather than an individual daily transaction.

**Example:**

```text
2012-11-02
```

**Type:** Date / Time

**Why is it important?**

`Date` is one of the most important columns for forecasting because sales can depend on:

- Year
- Month
- Week of the year
- Season
- Holidays
- Long-term trends

For Machine Learning models, it is often useful to extract additional features from this column, such as:

```text
Year
Month
Week
Quarter
```

---

### 4. `Weekly_Sales`

**Description:**  
Represents the total sales generated by a specific department in a specific store during a specific week.

**Example:**

```text
15234.50
```

**Type:** Numerical

**Role:** **Target Variable**

This is the value we want our model to predict.

For example:

```text
Store = 1
Dept = 1
Date = 2012-11-02

Weekly_Sales = 15432.10
```

In the `test.csv` file, this column is intentionally missing because the goal of the competition is to predict it.

---

### 5. `IsHoliday`

**Description:**  
Indicates whether the week contains a major holiday.

**Possible values:**

```text
True
False
```

**Type:** Boolean / Categorical

**Why is it important?**

Holiday periods can significantly change customer purchasing behavior and therefore affect sales.

The competition gives holiday weeks a weight of **5**, while normal weeks have a weight of **1** during evaluation.

The major holiday periods represented in the dataset include:

- Super Bowl
- Labor Day
- Thanksgiving
- Christmas

# Store Information

The following columns come from `stores.csv`.

---

### 6. `Type`

**Description:**  
Represents the type or format of the Walmart store.

There are three store types:

```text
A
B
C
```

**Type:** Categorical

**Why is it important?**

Different store types can have different:

- Sizes
- Customer volumes
- Product distributions
- Sales patterns

Therefore, `Type` can help the model distinguish between different kinds of stores.

---

### 7. `Size`

**Description:**  
Represents the size of the Walmart store.

It is measured as the store's available selling area.

**Type:** Numerical

**Why is it important?**

A larger store generally has the capacity to carry more products and serve more customers.

Therefore, `Size` can be useful for explaining differences in sales between stores.

---

# External / Regional Features

The following columns come from `features.csv`.

---

### 8. `Temperature`

**Description:**  
Represents the average temperature in the region during that week.

**Type:** Numerical

**Example:**

```text
Temperature = 72.5
```

**Why is it important?**

Weather can influence customer behavior and product demand.

For example:

- Very hot weather may increase demand for certain products.
- Cold weather may increase demand for winter-related products.
- Extreme weather may reduce store visits.

Therefore, temperature can potentially affect weekly sales.

---

### 9. `Fuel_Price`

**Description:**  
Represents the cost of fuel in the region during that week.

**Type:** Numerical

**Why is it important?**

Fuel prices can influence consumer behavior and transportation costs.

For example, higher fuel prices may affect:

- Customers' willingness to travel
- Transportation costs
- Overall consumer spending

Therefore, it can act as an external economic feature.

---

# Promotional Features

The dataset contains five Markdown columns:

```text
MarkDown1
MarkDown2
MarkDown3
MarkDown4
MarkDown5
```

---

### 10. `MarkDown1`

### 11. `MarkDown2`

### 12. `MarkDown3`

### 13. `MarkDown4`

### 14. `MarkDown5`

**Description:**  
These columns contain anonymized information related to Walmart's promotional markdown activities.

A markdown generally refers to reducing the price of products as part of a promotion or clearance strategy.

**Type:** Numerical

**Why are there five columns?**

The original dataset does not provide a clear public definition for each individual Markdown column. Instead, they represent different anonymized promotional variables.

Therefore, we should **not assume** that:

```text
MarkDown1 = percentage discount
MarkDown2 = number of products
...
```

unless additional documentation explicitly defines them.

**Important:**  
Markdown data is not available for all stores and all weeks, and missing values are represented as `NA`.

**Why are they important?**

Promotions can increase customer demand and therefore potentially increase weekly sales.

---

# Economic Features

### 15. `CPI`

**Full Name:** Consumer Price Index

**Description:**  
Measures changes in the general price level of goods and services in the economy.

**Type:** Numerical

**Why is it important?**

CPI provides information about the economic and price environment during a particular period.

Changes in prices can influence:

- Consumer purchasing power
- Product prices
- Consumer spending behavior

Therefore, CPI can help the model understand broader economic conditions.

---

### 16. `Unemployment`

**Description:**  
Represents the unemployment rate in the region.

**Type:** Numerical

**Example:**

```text
Unemployment = 7.5
```

**Why is it important?**

Unemployment can be related to consumers' purchasing power.

For example, higher unemployment may be associated with lower consumer spending, which could potentially affect retail sales.

# Important Relationship Between the Files

The original dataset is split across different files.

### `train.csv`

Contains:

```text
Store
Dept
Date
Weekly_Sales
IsHoliday
```

### `test.csv`

Contains:

```text
Store
Dept
Date
IsHoliday
```

The `Weekly_Sales` column is removed because it is the **target we need to predict**.

### `stores.csv`

Contains:

```text
Store
Type
Size
```

### `features.csv`

Contains:

```text
Store
Date
Temperature
Fuel_Price
MarkDown1
MarkDown2
MarkDown3
MarkDown4
MarkDown5
CPI
Unemployment
IsHoliday
```

These datasets can be merged using the appropriate keys. For example, `Store` connects store information to the sales/features data, while `Store + Date` connects weekly store-level features to the corresponding sales records.

## The Main Idea

The model is basically trying to learn:

> **Given a specific store, department, week, holiday status, store characteristics, promotional activity, weather, and economic conditions, how much will that department sell during that week?**

So conceptually:

```text
Store
   +
Department
   +
Time
   +
Holiday
   +
Store Characteristics
   +
Weather
   +
Fuel Price
   +
Promotions
   +
Economic Conditions
        ↓
   Machine Learning Model
        ↓
  Weekly_Sales Prediction
```

The most important thing to remember is that this is not simply a normal tabular regression problem. Because `Date` represents a sequence of weeks, the dataset also contains **time-series patterns**, such as trends and seasonality.
